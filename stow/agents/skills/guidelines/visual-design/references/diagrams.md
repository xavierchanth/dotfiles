# Diagrams

Start from the question the diagram answers, the necessary entities and relationships,
and the intended takeaway. Use the writing skill to develop meaning and explanatory
sequence when needed. Let that meaning determine composition.

Choose a clear visual grammar for nodes, connections, grouping, sequence, and annotations.
Make direction and relationship types distinguishable. Keep labels close to what they
describe and use alignment or proximity to reveal structure before adding containers.

Choose the level of abstraction deliberately. Separate views when combining levels or
relationships makes the diagram harder to understand. Preserve the connections necessary
to explain the idea while removing incidental implementation detail.

Route connections to reduce ambiguous crossings and make important paths easy to follow.
Give groups and emphasized elements weight according to meaning. Use color as a supporting
signal alongside labels, shape, line treatment, or position.

Inspect at the size used in the destination artifact. Check label legibility, connection
endpoints, direction, grouping, and the conclusion a viewer is likely to draw. For a
progressive reveal, keep entities and visual meanings stable across steps.

## Editable Source

Use Mermaid for diagrams that fit its structured layouts, and Excalidraw for freer
composition. Keep the editable source canonical and regenerate PNGs for documents and
slides. Read [rendering](diagram-rendering.md) for setup, commands, and editing guidance.

Keep targeted edits to the source rather than editing exported images. Inspect each
render for clipping, connections, labels, and legibility at its destination size.
