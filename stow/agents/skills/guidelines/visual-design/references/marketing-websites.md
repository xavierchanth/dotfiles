# Marketing Websites

Start from the narrative and message architecture: audience, purpose, intended takeaway,
core message, supporting themes, evidence, and any useful next step. Use the writing
skill when those foundations need development before drafting the website.

Translate message priorities into visual presence and page relationships. A site may
need a linear story, multiple entry points, comparison, or progressive depth. Choose
the composition according to how visitors need to understand and explore the material.

Give the opening a deliberate visual role. It might express the core idea through
typography, demonstrate the product, establish a recognizable situation, or introduce
distinctive imagery. Let the subject and message determine the treatment.

Vary section composition according to communicative purpose. Place proof where it
supports a claim, show mechanisms where they aid understanding, and connect concise
messages to deeper material. Give calls to action presence appropriate to visitor
readiness and the page's purpose.

Preserve identity and message priorities on compact and wide screens. Read
[screen adaptation](screen-adaptation.md) and inspect narrative order, image crops,
type scale, and section rhythm across both. Judge the page with representative copy,
rather than relying on decorative placeholders to carry the design.
