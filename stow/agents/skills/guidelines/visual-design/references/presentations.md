# Presentations

Start from the established slide purposes, argument or explanation, and supporting
content. Use the writing skill for that architecture when needed. Determine whether
the deck supports a speaker, must stand alone, or needs both forms.

Compose each slide to make its intended contribution perceptible. Choose visual weight
for the conclusion, explanation, evidence, or relationship the audience needs. Adapt
density to viewing conditions and available reading time.

For investor decks, give the thesis and material evidence clear presence. For sales
decks, foreground the buyer-relevant situation, value, and demonstration. For technical
decks, make mechanisms and relationships legible at the audience's level of understanding.
These purposes guide treatment rather than impose fixed slide layouts.

Build continuity across slides with consistent meanings, typography, and image treatment.
Vary composition and density to support pacing. Use progressive disclosure where it
helps explanation, and preserve a comprehensible static form when the deck must be read.

Read [diagrams](diagrams.md) for technical relationships. Maintain readable annotations
and scales in charts and diagrams at presentation size. Review slides together for rhythm
and individually for legibility, then inspect the delivered format for layout changes.
