# Screen Adaptation

Establish the supported range from the artifact's use. Marketing websites normally
need compact and wide layouts; application coverage follows the product's distribution
and workflows. Available space and input conditions matter more than device labels alone.

Identify what must remain perceptible: primary content or action, essential context,
relationships, and identity. Decide how each region reflows, stacks, condenses, changes
presentation, or moves into contextual access as space changes.

Choose transitions where the composition stops working. Preserve meaningful reading
order and comparison relationships when rearranging regions. For dense tables or
canvases, choose an appropriate bounded view or navigation treatment rather than
shrinking everything beyond readability.

Tune typography, line length, spacing, and imagery across sizes. Reconsider crops and
alignment so the subject remains clear. Use extra width to improve relationships and
balance rather than stretching every element.

Inspect compact, wide, and intermediate layouts with realistic content. Account for
text enlargement, long strings, relevant input controls, and changing content. Verify
that adaptation retains useful context and recognizable actions.
