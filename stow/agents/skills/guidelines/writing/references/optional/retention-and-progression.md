# Retention and Progression

Use when engagement, openings, pacing, anticipation, or reveals need attention.
Help the audience see why continuing is worthwhile and reward that attention.

## Choose the Opening's Job

An opening can establish prerequisite knowledge, make a situation recognizable,
introduce a bold idea, pose a consequential question, or foreshadow a useful discovery.
Choose according to the audience's starting position and the piece's purpose.

## Plan Information Release

Identify what the audience knows at each point, what they are curious about, and what
the next section contributes. Introduce questions whose answers matter. Decide which
answers should be immediate and which benefit from preparation.

Use delayed reveals when the intervening material deepens their meaning. An essay
may build toward an insight; a guide often benefits from showing the destination early.
Give useful intermediate insights and resolve the expectations the piece creates.

## Sustain Progress

Let each section advance the thinking. Vary explanation, concrete examples, reflection,
and visual material where that helps understanding. Use transitions to connect ideas
or expose the next question, rather than repeatedly announcing what comes next.

Inspect where momentum stalls, a tangent loses its connection, or a promise remains
unfulfilled. Propose relocating, condensing, or strengthening material according to
the section's job. Optimize for worthwhile attention, not simply delayed access to an answer.
