# Voice and Transcript Editing

Treat the user's ideas and voice as anchors while making the expression readable.
Use supplied material to understand vocabulary, directness, humor, emphasis, and
conviction. Different artifacts can adopt different editorial stances without losing voice.

## Organize Before Replacing

Reorder ideas, group related material, and identify repetition before proposing
replacement sentences. Keep source excerpts available so editing remains traceable.
Preserve characteristic phrases and asides when they contribute meaning or personality.

## Turn Speech into Readable Language

Identify incidental filler, false starts, abandoned clauses, and transcription errors.
Propose removing them while retaining intentional conversational rhythm. Split winding
sentences, reconnect interrupted clauses, and restore clear subjects or references
where necessary. Preserve the core words where they work.

Distinguish a spoken hesitation from meaningful uncertainty. Retain the latter.
When an ambiguous transcript could change the idea, ask what the user intended.

## Make the Editorial Pass Reviewable

Batch related proposals. Use this comparison when it helps:

| Source wording | Proposed wording | Reason |
|---|---|---|

Reasons can include clarity, reading rhythm, perspective, register, tense, or
terminology consistency. Identify connective text and substantive additions separately
from edits to supplied language. Offer the user a meaningful opportunity to respond
before incorporating substantive changes, following the collaborative process in SKILL.md.

Preserve purposeful shifts between first-person experience and direct address.
Check that edits leave the claim, implications, and strength of conviction intact.
