# Handoff

Transfer the intended outcome, relevant decisions and rationale, constraints, dependencies, available artifacts, authority, escalation boundary, and expected return. Prefer links and source locations over transcripts or broad dumps. Mark assumptions and superseded decisions when they affect the next agent.

When Teams have an established dependency or handoff, the producing Team sends the relevant result directly to the consuming Team through the harness's task-messaging mechanism. Include the evidence and canonical artifacts needed for the next step, the already-authorized action, and any remaining blocker. Use the resolved destination task and host when needed, with Project context to distinguish similar names or shared workspace paths.

The consuming Team integrates the input with its current assignment and judges whether it enables or changes the next authorized action. For an actionable handoff, continue that work within scope, coordinating directly with peers as needed, then report the useful outcome or a concrete blocker to the appropriate recipients. An acknowledgment can confirm receipt and the next step while work proceeds; it is not the stopping point for an actionable assignment. A purely informational update may only need to update context; avoid inventing work or restarting completed actions. Respect explicit holds and approval boundaries, and ask only for consequential missing information while continuing independent work.

Reconcile uncertain delivery before retrying. Acknowledgment establishes receipt, not completion or approval. Track the outstanding dependency until its useful result is delivered.

A handoff transfers information and work already authorized within the recipient's scope; it grants no new authority to publish, merge, deploy, change ownership, or broaden scope. Resolve routine details between the Teams. Bring unresolved ownership, priorities, scope, or authority to Xavier. When the destination or contract is unclear, establish it before routing operational work to another Team.

Prefer direct push delivery along the agreed chain. Use status inspection and bounded waiting mainly to reconcile state, recover missing or uncertain handoffs, or satisfy an immediate synchronous dependency.

## Focused collaboration with Xavier

Use the Team task as the direct place for Xavier to review artifacts, resolve specialist decisions, or work through an attended workflow. Keep the Team's detailed context there and report a coherent result to Xavier in that task. Direct peer handoffs remain subject to the agreed scope and authority.
