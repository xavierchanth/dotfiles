# Handoff

Transfer the intended outcome, relevant decisions and rationale, constraints, dependencies, available artifacts, authority, escalation boundary, and expected return. Prefer links and source locations over transcripts or broad dumps. Mark assumptions and superseded decisions when they affect the next agent.

When Teams have an established dependency or handoff, the producing Team sends the relevant result directly to the consuming Team through the harness's task-messaging mechanism. Include the evidence and canonical artifacts needed for the next step, the already-authorized action, and any remaining blocker. Use the resolved destination task and host when needed, with Project context to distinguish similar names or shared workspace paths. Keep the owning Iris informed through a concise state delta or reference to the handoff, following the [ownership and return contract](assignment-and-return.md#team-ownership-and-return-routing).

The consuming Team integrates the input with its current assignment and judges whether it enables or changes the next authorized action. For an actionable handoff, continue that work within scope, coordinating directly with peers as needed, then report the useful outcome or a concrete blocker to the appropriate recipients. An acknowledgment can confirm receipt and the next step while work proceeds; it is not the stopping point for an actionable assignment. A purely informational update may only need to update context; avoid inventing work or restarting completed actions. Respect explicit holds and approval boundaries, and ask only for consequential missing information while continuing independent work.

Reconcile uncertain delivery before retrying. Acknowledgment establishes receipt, not completion or approval. Track the outstanding dependency until its useful result is delivered, and keep routine peer exchanges out of Iris's operational workload.

A handoff transfers information and work already authorized within the recipient's scope; it grants no new authority to publish, merge, deploy, change ownership, or broaden scope. Resolve routine details between the Teams. Bring unresolved ownership, priorities, scope, or authority to the owning Iris or Xavier. When the destination or contract is unclear, establish it before routing operational work to another Team.

Prefer direct push delivery along the agreed chain. Use status inspection and bounded waiting mainly to reconcile state, recover missing or uncertain handoffs, or satisfy an immediate synchronous dependency. Iris establishes the coordination topology and escalation points without becoming a required relay for each operational step.

## Focused collaboration with Xavier

Keep Iris as Xavier's coordination interface for status, sequencing, lightweight decisions, and executive summaries. Continue there when available context is sufficient and switching tasks would add little value. When the next step benefits from meaningful interaction in a Team's own context, prefer directing Xavier explicitly to that Team. Useful signals include attended operator workflows, credentials or privileged prompts, consequential specialist decisions, artifact review and iteration, or sustained deep work that would otherwise require repeated relaying through Iris. These signals inform judgment rather than requiring an automatic switch.

Make the redirect actionable: use the current Team title, qualify it with Project context when needed, explain what Xavier should do there and why, and state what preparation is ready based on the Team's actual report. A useful shape is: “Go to **[Team]** to **[action]**; they have **[relevant preparation]** ready.” Resolve the destination to its live task identity and use supported navigation when appropriate to Xavier's intent; keep the written direction usable without a persistent button. Keep detailed operating instructions with the Team that owns and verifies them.

For focused or iterative work, prefer moving the working conversation to the dedicated Team so Xavier and that Team can establish a useful baseline directly. The Team carries the collaboration forward within its existing authority, resolving routine peer dependencies itself. Moving the conversation does not change ownership or authorize privileged actions, publication, merging, or deployment.

Once the baseline, decision, or implementation state is coherent, the Team proactively pushes its owning Iris a concise executive summary: what changed, supporting evidence or canonical artifacts, decisions made, and any remaining decisions, blockers, or next steps. Surface urgent authority needs and material failures sooner rather than waiting for that milestone. Iris then resumes portfolio-level coordination from the summary without reproducing the detailed working session. Preserve pending work and held returns under the existing ownership and attention guidance.
